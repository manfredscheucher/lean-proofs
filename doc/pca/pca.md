# Pca: triangles in arrangements of pseudocircles

Library `Pca` (default target), root `CombinatorialGeometry/Pca.lean`. Model: `model.md`.

## What is proved

For every simple intersecting arrangement `A : Arrangement n D` of `n` pseudocircles:

* Basic counts (`Pca/Derived.lean`): `n(n−1)` crossings (`card_X`), `n(n−1) + 2` cells
  (`card_cells`), `2(n−1)` crossings on every curve, connectivity (`connected`).
* Counting identities (`Pca/Excess.lean`): `3 p_3 = ∑ t(x)` and the excess identities.
* Digon lemma (`Pca/Digon.lean`, `n ≥ 4`): a crossing is a corner of at most one digon, a corner
  of a digon touches at most one triangle, hence `def ≥ 2 p_2` (`two_p2_le_deficiency`).
* Fan lemma (`Pca/Fan.lean`, `n ≥ 4`): a crossing has at most three triangles (`t_le_three`); if
  it has three, they form a fan (`fan_lemma`), with consequences (a) to (e) (`Fan.cons_a` …
  `Fan.cons_e`).
* Triangle flip (`Pca/Flip.lean`): the flip of any triangle is an arrangement, and the seven
  cells around it are distinct (`flip_seven_distinct`).
* Upper bound (`Pca/Potential.lean`, `Pca/Main.lean`, `n ≥ 4`), using the digon axiom:
  * `potential_lemma`: `|X'| + p_2 ≤ 2n − 2`,
  * `main_theorem`: `3 p_3 ≤ 4·C(n,2) + 2n − 2 − 3 p_2`, also over `ℚ` (`main_theorem_rat`),
  * `main_digon_free`: without digons, `p_3 ≤ 4/3·C(n,2) + (2n−2)/3`,
  * `cor_main`, `cor_main_floor`: `p_3 ≤ ⌊2(n²−1)/3⌋`.
* Sweeping lemma (`Pca/SweepLemma.lean`): `sweepFirstMove`, `sweepTwoCells`. On each side of a
  curve there are two digons or triangles with a side on it. Proof in `PcaSweep` (`sweep.md`).
* Lower bounds (`Pca/LowerBound.lean`):
  * `in_out` (`n ≥ 3`): all digons with a side on `C` lie on the same side of `C`,
  * `two_le_tC`: every curve has at least two triangles with a side on it,
  * `lower_bound_two_thirds` (`n ≥ 3`): `2n ≤ 3 p_3`,
  * `sh_bound`: `4n ≤ 2 p_2 + 3 p_3`, and `lower_bound_digon_free`: without digons `4n ≤ 3 p_3`.
* `TriangleConjecture` (`p_3 ≥ n − 1` for `n ≥ 3`) is stated as a `Prop`. It is open, not
  assumed, and nothing uses it.

All declarations are in namespace `Pca.Arrangement` (the fan lemmas in `Pca.Arrangement.Fan`).

## Axioms

One: `Pca.digon_bound` (`Pca/DigonAxiom.lean`), an intersecting arrangement of `n ≥ 3`
pseudocircles has at most `2n − 2` digons (Ackerman, Damásdi, Keszegh, Pinchasi, Raffay). It is
used by `potential_lemma` and everything after it in `Pca/Main.lean`. Everything else, including
the sweeping lemma and the lower bounds, uses only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.

## Sources

* S. Felsner, M. Scheucher, *Arrangements of Pseudocircles: Triangles and Drawings*,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.06449 (setting, lower bounds, use of the sweeping lemma).
* The upper bound on `p_3` is a new result by M. Scheucher, not yet published.
* Digon theorem: E. Ackerman, G. Damásdi, B. Keszegh, R. Pinchasi, R. Raffay,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10023.
* Sweeping lemma: J. Snoeyink, J. Hershberger, *Sweeping arrangements of curves*, DIMACS Series 6
  (1991), 309–349.

## Deviations from the papers

* A `k`-cell is defined by the length of its face orbit. That it has `k` distinct corners and
  sides is proved for digons and triangles only, which is all the proofs need.
* The argument that the seven cells of a flip are distinct uses a closed curve crossing a
  pseudocircle once. Here it is the combinatorial Jordan theorem `exists_side`.
* Small gaps in the paper proofs are closed: several steps of the fan lemma use that the two
  neighbours of a crossing along a curve are distinct; `in_out` has a case (both digons formed
  with the same curve) that the paper does not treat and that needs `n ≥ 3`; the lower bound
  `4n ≤ 2 p_2 + 3 p_3` needs two cells on each side of a curve, which is the form proved.
* The potential argument flips the middle triangle of a fan at an arbitrary crossing of `X'` and
  inducts on `2n − 2 − p_2`, which is the paper's "at most `n − 1` flips".

## Test instances

Library `PcaInstances` checks all arrangements with `n ≤ 4` by `native_decide`: the axioms, that
each map is the arrangement of its fingerprint, the values of `p_2`, `p_3`, `|X'|`, the counting
identities and the statement of the sweeping lemma. Negative tests reject maps that violate
exactly one of the axioms 5, 6, 7. The instance files are generated by
`scripts/pca/fingerprints_to_lean.py`.
